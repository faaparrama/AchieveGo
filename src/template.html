THE FRAMEWORK

Why a continuing cycle?

Our bibliometric study mapped talent-identification research across education, sport, and management, including work that extends beyond initial selection (Parra-Martínez & Wai, 2023). The actiotope model treats gifted performance as developing through the interaction of a learner's actions, goals, and environment (Ziegler & Phillipson, 2012). A situated account of gifted underachievement examines person × task × situation dynamics and links diagnosis with intervention (Dai & Desmet, 2026).

Figure 1 brings these ideas together as a proposed cycle. AI has two bounded roles: it can surface evidence, options, and uncertainty for team decisions, and it can scaffold learners' inquiry, feedback, and creation during enrichment. Educators and learners retain responsibility for goals, interpretation, and judging whether assisted work becomes durable learning. The cycle guides this prototype; its learner-to-intervention matching rules still require validation.

The cycle in six steps
  1. Identify and understand.
  2. Plan enrichment and AI roles.
  3. Allocate opportunity and support.
  4. Enable and monitor participation.
  5. Assess learning and transfer.
  6. Revise and reallocate.

Review goals, educational and learning capital, context, and access throughout.

DESCRIPTIVE RESEARCH

Profiles as one source of information

Our latent profile analysis describes 48,789 students across 108 Dutch schools using three reasoning and three psychosocial indicators. Motivation and well-being distinguish the profiles more than the reasoning measures in this sample.

The table reports group means in standardized units. These patterns do not diagnose or classify individual students, and their usefulness in US settings remains untested.

THE UNTESTED MATCH

From evidence to a learner's plan

The starter library does not establish that a particular profile and diagnosis combination responds best to a specific intervention. Educators and specialists must examine applicability, feasible delivery, and the learner's goals before selecting an option.

Inspectable rules organize this review. The prototype does not diagnose students or estimate individual treatment effects.

SHARED DEVELOPMENT

Research into practice

Researchers appraise evidence and test matching hypotheses. Educators and specialists determine what schools can deliver. Learners and families identify goals and access barriers. Engineers make sources, decisions, and revisions traceable.

The next studies should examine usability, evidence appraisal, and whether access to the library or profile-informed planning improves decisions and outcomes.
